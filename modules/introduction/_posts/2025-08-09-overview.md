# The Discipline of Network Engineering

Network Engineering is a discipline which focuses to design, implement and operate communication networks, allowing devices to communicate over various physical mediums. A network can be as small as a point-to-point link connecting two routers, or the big "I" Internet, a network of networks. 

Network design is primarily driven by organizational needs, which vary wildly. Thus, Network Engineering is grouped into various sub-disciplines covering general use cases, which include:
- Enterprise and Campus 
- Data Center 
- Service Provider 
- High Frequency Trading 
- Research / High Performance Computing 
- Military
- And more....

Like most engineering disciplines, skills / expertise at one of these does not guarantee expertise in another, and the day-to-day duties of a Enterprise Network Entgineer differ greatly from that of a HPC Network Engineer. 

As an example, the business case for a Network Engineer in HPC may be supporting thousands of compute nodes on a single LAN, aiming to reduce network latency as much as possible. This is very different from a Network Engineer at a ISP, who may be responsible for connecting users to the Internet across the globe. The business case and technologies used to solve the first problem are not the ones used to solve the second, despite both being Network Engineering. 

# Network Engineering in the 21st Century

The modern Internet and networking technology consists of intentional design decisions, made at a specific point in time, to meet a specific use case. When getting into the headspace of Network Engineering, it is useful to think about specific technologies, knobs, or patterns as addressing a specific problem faced at that time. Many of these problems (and their solutions) remain relevant to this day. But often, you will see decisions within technologies that make little sense in the modern day (IPv4 address space, for example), however at the time were very viable and met the need of the time. Other technologies such as MPLS, met the need of their time, and transformed as that need went away, to address broader problems.
